How AURA Works

Not a related-products widget. An agentic system that watches how you learn, reasons about it, and recommends real courses — with a story about why they matter.

The agent loop

Six stages, one compiled LangGraph state machine. Every recommendation passes through all of them.

01

Observe

Views, searches, clicks, wishlists, syllabus opens and dwell time are batched in your browser and flushed without ever blocking the page.

02

Understand

A Mesh-LLM node reads your recent activity and infers interests, skill level and intent — a living profile that updates as you explore.

03

Retrieve

Your intent becomes a semantic query over the vector catalog. Prerequisite-aware filtering keeps candidates realistic — nothing is invented.

04

Evaluate

A second reasoning pass scores candidates 0–100 and reranks them. Below 60, the agent broadens the search and retrieves again — up to two refetches.

05

Persuade

An AIDA-style narrative is written for your cognitive style — analytical, social, motivational or practical — explaining why these courses, now.

06

Critique & store

A validator checks length and grounding; if the story doesn't name your courses, it's rewritten. The result is stored and served to your dashboard.

Why it feels personal

Four design decisions that separate this from a popularity list.

Triggers

Smart about when to think

The agent runs only when behavior actually changes — high-intent actions, search bursts, staleness — behind a 10-minute cooldown. No LLM call per click.

Grounding

Real catalog, real order

Dual-write keeps SQL and the vector store in sync, and prerequisite-aware reranking means you're never sold a course you can't take yet.

Persuasion

The same course, argued your way

ROI and depth for analytical readers, community for social learners, momentum for motivators — the framing adapts, not just the list.

Memory

It remembers, and follows up

Your profile persists across sessions, and a 9:00 AM digest can recap yesterday's interests with a fresh narrative while you're away.

AURA only uses your on-platform activity. Nothing external, ever.
